# Content Needed From the Gym Owner

Everything on the website right now is placeholder text — realistic-sounding, but invented. This is the list of real information needed to replace it.

You don't need to write polished copy. Rough notes, bullet points, or voice memos are fine — the wording can be tidied up afterwards. What matters is that the facts, names, prices and times are correct.

Tick things off as you collect them.

---

## 1. The basics

- [ ] **Coach's full name** — as they want it to appear publicly *(currently a made-up name)*
- [ ] **Gym or business name** — the official name people should search for
- [ ] **Job title** — e.g. "Boxing Coach", "Head Coach", "Strength & Boxing Coach". Two or three words, appears under the gym name in the top corner of every page.

---

## 2. How people get in touch

- [ ] **Phone number** — the one that actually gets answered
- [ ] **Email address** — where enquiries should land
- [ ] Should the phone number be shown publicly, or would they rather people only use email?

---

## 3. Where the gym is

- [ ] **Street address, line 1** — building name, unit number, street
- [ ] **Town/city and postcode**
- [ ] Any **directions or parking notes** worth adding? (e.g. "round the back of the retail park", "free parking after 6pm", "nearest station is 5 minutes' walk")

The address is also used to generate a "open in Google Maps" link, so it needs to be accurate enough for Maps to find it.

---

## 4. The main headline

This is the first thing visitors read, in very large letters at the top of the home page.

- [ ] **One-line headline** — short and punchy, ideally under about ten words. *(The placeholder is "Learn to box. Get seriously fit. Earn every round.")*
- [ ] **A short paragraph underneath** — two or three sentences saying who the gym is for and what makes it different. Roughly 40 words.

If the coach has a saying or a phrase they always use with new people, that often works better than anything written from scratch.

---

## 5. Experience and qualifications

- [ ] **How many years have they been coaching?** (a number)
- [ ] **Roughly how many people have they trained?** (a rounded number is fine — it's shown as "400+")
- [ ] **Four short credentials**, one line each. These appear as a list. Things worth including:
  - Coaching qualifications and the awarding body
  - Years in the sport, and how many of those coaching
  - Competitive record, if they have one and want it shown
  - Safety credentials — background check, first aid, concussion training, insurance

Anything that requires a certificate should be double-checked before it goes on the site, since it's a public claim about qualifications.

---

## 6. Training options and prices

There are currently four options on the site. They can be changed, renamed, removed, or added to — but for each one, we need:

- [ ] **Name of the option** — e.g. "1-on-1 Coaching", "Small Group Classes"
- [ ] **Price** — the headline number
- [ ] **What the price covers** — e.g. "per session", "per class", "per month"
- [ ] **A two-sentence description** — what it is and who it suits
- [ ] **Four bullet points** — what's included, any discounts, block-booking deals, anything worth knowing before signing up

The four placeholder options are: private one-to-one coaching, small group classes, fight preparation for people with a bout booked, and youth boxing for ages 8–15. Are those the right four?

Also needed:

- [ ] **Which one should be marked "Most popular"?** (One option gets highlighted — it draws the eye, so it's worth choosing deliberately.)
- [ ] **Are there concessions?** Student, over-65, unemployed, family rates?
- [ ] **Is the first session genuinely free?** This promise appears in several places, including the button in the top corner of every page. If it's a paid taster or a discounted first session instead, that needs changing everywhere.
- [ ] **What's included in every membership at no extra cost?** Six items are currently listed — things like loan gloves for the first month, a written plan, access to open gym hours, kit storage. Which of these are real?

⚠️ **Currency check:** the placeholder prices are in dollars but the placeholder address is in the UK. Whichever is right, prices and phone number format both need to match the real location.

---

## 7. The class timetable

Needed for **each day of the week**:

- [ ] **Start and finish time** of each session
- [ ] **Name of the session** — e.g. "Beginners Boxing", "Open Gym"
- [ ] **Who it's for** — beginner, intermediate, advanced, all levels, or youth
- [ ] **Any conditions** — e.g. "invitation only", "headgear required", "term time only"

Also worth confirming:

- [ ] **Are classes drop-in, or does everything need booking?** The site currently says group classes are drop-in and only private sessions need booking.
- [ ] **What happens on bank holidays and over Christmas?**
- [ ] **Do the times change in summer or during school holidays?**

If there's already a timetable on a poster, a wall, or a Facebook post, a photo of it is enough.

---

## 8. Reviews from members

Three quotes are shown on the home page and the training page.

- [ ] **Three genuine testimonials.** For each one:
  - What they said (two or three sentences)
  - Their name, or first name and last initial
  - A short detail — e.g. "Beginners class, 8 months", "Parent, Youth Boxing"

⚠️ **Important:** these must be real, and the people quoted need to have agreed to appear on the website. Invented reviews are both dishonest and, in most places, illegal. If there aren't three yet, it's better to show one real one — or none — than to make them up. Existing Google or Facebook reviews can be reused if the reviewer is happy with it.

---

## 9. Common questions

Six questions and answers are currently on the training page, covering: starting with no experience, whether you need your own kit, whether sparring is compulsory, what to bring to a first session, being too old or unfit, and whether there's a contract.

- [ ] **Confirm or correct each of the six answers** — these are guesses about how this particular gym runs, and some are almost certainly wrong.
- [ ] **What else do people ask before their first session?** The coach will know the three or four questions that come up every single time. Those are the valuable ones.

---

## 10. Social media

- [ ] **Instagram** page link, if there is one
- [ ] **Facebook** page link, if there is one
- [ ] **YouTube** channel link, if there is one

Any of these can be left out — empty ones simply won't appear. Only include accounts that are actually kept up to date; a dead account linked from the website looks worse than no link at all.

---

## 11. Photographs

The site has no real photos yet — there's a placeholder pattern where the main image should be, and a labelled empty box on the About page.

- [ ] **A photo of the coach** — upright/portrait shape works best
- [ ] **A main image for the top of the home page** — wide/landscape, ideally the gym itself, a training session, or the coach on the pads
- [ ] **A handful of general gym photos** — the space, the ring, people training

Photos taken on a recent phone are perfectly good. Two things matter more than camera quality: decent light, and permission from anyone recognisable in the shot — particularly important for any photo involving under-18s, where written parental consent is needed.

---

## 12. The coach's story

The About page has a short life story and a timeline of five moments — starting out, competing, first coaching job, qualifying, and opening the current gym. All of it is invented.

- [ ] **How did they get into boxing?**
- [ ] **Did they compete? At what level, and roughly when?**
- [ ] **When and why did they start coaching?**
- [ ] **When did they qualify, and in what?**
- [ ] **When did this gym open?**
- [ ] **What do they want people to understand about how they coach?** The site currently claims four principles — fundamentals before combinations, nobody gets embarrassed, sparring is always optional, and honest feedback. Are those actually theirs?

A fifteen-minute conversation, recorded, will produce better material here than asking them to write it down.

---

## 13. Practical set-up decisions

These aren't content, but they're needed before the site can go live and only the owner can answer them.

- [ ] **Web address** — do they already own a domain name? If not, one needs buying.
- [ ] **Where should enquiry form submissions go?** The contact form isn't connected to an inbox yet. It needs an email address, and a service to deliver them.
- [ ] **Who checks that inbox, and how quickly?** The site currently promises a same-day reply. If that's not realistic, it should say something else.
- [ ] **Are there terms, a privacy policy, or gym rules** that should be linked in the footer? A site collecting names and email addresses generally needs a privacy notice.
- [ ] **Is the business insured, and is there a policy number** worth displaying?

---

## Where to start

If the full list feels like a lot, these five unblock the most work:

1. Real name, gym name, phone, email and address
2. The class timetable
3. The list of training options with real prices
4. Confirmation of whether the first session is free
5. A photo of the coach and one of the gym

Everything else can be filled in gradually. But **nothing invented should stay on the site once it's public** — particularly the qualifications, the testimonials, and the prices.
